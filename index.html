01 Scientific rationale

Comparing people with their own history
makes data more useful.

A single reading may be ordinary or unusual depending on the person, the moment and what came before.

With appropriate permissions, NeuroSuite brings together data from smartphones, wearables and other compatible sensors. It places readings on a timeline and builds a personal reference: typical movement, activity patterns and recurring rhythms.

When something differs from that reference, the system looks for related changes in other available signals. The aim is to give carers and professionals observations they can review in context, rather than a stream of isolated numbers.

03 Frontal EEG

Sensors on the forehead.
What can we actually observe?

EEG records electrical activity detectable at the scalp. Even a few frontal electrodes can show changes in brain rhythms across repeated measurements. The key is comparing recordings made under similar conditions, rather than assigning meaning to a single number.

1. Rest and attention

Eyes closed, during a task or after a demanding day, EEG rhythms may differ. We can measure changes in alpha, theta and beta band power over time without automatically labelling attention or emotions.

2. Your own starting point

Three short recordings using the same device under comparable conditions can help establish an initial reference. Later sessions can be compared with it: what rose, what fell and whether a difference repeats.

3. Brain and body together

When an EEG recording changes, NeuroSuite can place it alongside sleep, movement and heart-rate data when available and authorised. For example, a change after reduced sleep may have a different context from a recurring change measured at rest.

4. A report people can read

The goal is a clear summary: “Compared with reference sessions, this recording differs in the alpha band; signal quality was checked; a comparable repeat measurement is recommended.” This is an illustration of the intended presentation, not a validated clinical finding.

Signal quality matters: eye movements, forehead muscles, electrode contact and electrical noise can distort recordings. A few frontal EEG channels cannot read thoughts or, by themselves, diagnose disease, predict seizures or establish mental states. Specific clinical uses require evaluation with professionals and appropriate studies.

05 Responsibility

Protected by design.
Shaped for real use.

Personal, care, workplace, retail and research domains remain separated by purpose, environment, permissions and access. NeuroSuite applies data minimisation, provenance, revocation, auditability and security by design; its software, models, methods, identity and know-how remain protected intellectual property. Some verticals and integrations include a finalisation and validation phase within each specific use case: configuration around the partner’s processes, environments and operational needs, followed by verification in real operating contexts. The capabilities presented describe the ecosystem’s application scope; regulated, clinical or safety-critical uses require the applicable governance, evidence and authorisations.
